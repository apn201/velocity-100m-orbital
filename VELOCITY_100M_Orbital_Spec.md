## 0. The idea

Take every tracked piece of orbital debris and every spent rocket stage. Put them on their real orbits around Earth. Hold a qualifying round: raise the speed threshold and eliminate the slow ones, heat by heat, until five remain. Then straighten their orbits into a 100 m track and race them, with the ISS in lane 6 as the reference.

### The physics surprise that makes it a story

In a circular orbit, speed depends only on altitude. Everything in low orbit, ISS included, moves at 7.3 to 7.9 km/s. If the field were only LEO debris, the final would be a five-way tie with the ISS, and nobody would care.

The winners come from somewhere else: spent upper stages left in geostationary transfer orbits. These ellipses run from a few hundred km up to 36,000 km and beyond, and at the bottom of the ellipse, at perigee, they move at about 10.2 to 10.5 km/s. Roughly 35% faster than the ISS.

So the qualifying round has a visible plot twist. As the threshold rises past about 7.9 km/s, the whole dense shell of LEO debris around Earth disappears at once, and what remains is a few hundred long, thin ellipses that were hidden in the noise. The fastest objects near Earth are rocket stages that finished their job years ago and are still falling.

### The race

At perigee speed, 100 m takes about 10 ms. The ISS takes about 13 ms. Rough numbers, to be replaced by real data in S0:

- Winner, ~10.4 km/s: 9.6 ms
- ISS, 7.66 km/s: 13.1 ms. When the winner crosses the line, the ISS is at about 73 m.
- The starter's pistol: sound travels about 3.3 m in 9.6 ms. It hasn't reached lane 2.
- Lane 7, world record pace, 9.58 s for 100 m. Reaction time alone is about 0.15 s, so the runner never leaves the blocks during the race.

### Final card

```
WINNER: <OBJECT NAME>
LAUNCHED <YEAR>.
NOBODY IS STEERING IT.
```

Name and year come from the data. Alternates if the winner's data suggests better: `STILL FALLING.` or `TOP SPEED REACHED <N> YEARS AGO.`

## 2. Data

### 2.1 Sources

CelesTrak GP data. There is no full-catalogue download and no rocket-body group, so the field is assembled from a few queries, each downloaded once and cached:

- `gp.php?NAME=DEB&FORMAT=json` - everything with DEB in its name
- `gp.php?NAME=R/B&FORMAT=json` - rocket bodies
- `gp.php?GROUP=stations&FORMAT=json` - for the ISS (ZARYA, catalogue number 25544)

Respect the usage rules: one download per group per 2-hour update, cache locally, and stop on any 301, 403, 404 or 500. Never retry in a loop.

Check in S0 that `NAME=DEB` and `NAME=R/B` return what we expect. If they don't, fall back to Space-Track (free account): `gp` class, `OBJECT_TYPE` in (`DEBRIS`, `ROCKET BODY`), `DECAY_DATE` null. Space-Track's user agreement restricts redistribution, so if Space-Track is used, the raw catalogue is never committed or published. Only the scripts and the film are.

### 2.2 tools/fetch.py

Downloads the queries above into `data/raw/` (gitignored), with a timestamp. It refuses to download again if the cache is under 2 hours old.

### 2.3 tools/rank.py

Ordinary Python, outside Blender. Uses `sgp4` only to sanity-check, and plain two-body maths for the ranking.

For each object:
- `mu = 398600.4418 km^3/s^2`, `R_earth = 6378.137 km`
- semi-major axis `a` from mean motion
- `r_p = a(1-e)`, `r_a = a(1+e)`
- perigee speed `v_p = sqrt(mu * (2/r_p - 1/a))`
- launch year from the international designator

Filters, all logged with counts:
- epoch within 30 days of the chosen epoch
- `0 <= e < 1`
- perigee altitude over 120 km
- no duplicates by catalogue number

Outputs:
- `data/field.json`: one row per object - catalogue number, name, type, launch year, Keplerian elements at epoch, `v_p`, perigee and apogee altitude
- `data/heats.json`: thresholds and survivor counts per heat (section 3.2)
- `data/finalists.json`: top 5 by `v_p`, plus the ISS with the same fields
- `data/race.json`: per lane, 100 m time in ms, distance covered at the winner's finish, gap to winner, sound distance at the winner's finish

Epoch: `2026-10-31T00:00:00Z`, or the most recent common epoch if the data is older. The epoch is stated on screen.

### 2.4 Honesty rules

- The race is a flying start through perigee, at each object's perigee speed, in the Earth-centred inertial frame. The HUD says so: `FLYING START / PERIGEE SPEED / EARTH-CENTRED`.
- The display orbits are two-body Keplerian propagation from the epoch elements. Label: `ORBITS: TWO-BODY, FOR DISPLAY`. Accurate enough to look right for a few revolutions. Not a conjunction tool.
- Lanes are a cinematic straightening of each object's real perigee arc. They do not claim the objects share an orbit.
- Every number on screen comes from `field.json`, `race.json` or a constant listed in `sources.md`: speed of sound 343 m/s at 20 C, the 100 m world record 9.58 s, reaction time ~0.15 s.
- The world-record lane shows a generic wireframe sprinter labelled `WORLD RECORD PACE (9.58 s)`. No named person, no likeness.

## 3. Storyboard

28 s, 672 frames. Timings live in `data/timeline.json`.

### Segment 0 - Field (0.0-2.0 s, frames 1-48)

- Black. Wireframe globe fades in, the v2 globe builder reused.
- All objects appear as points on their orbits, moving in time-lapse at about one LEO revolution per 8 s of screen time.
- HUD:
  - `VELOCITY // 100 M ORBITAL`
  - `FIELD: <N> TRACKED OBJECTS`
  - `DEBRIS + ROCKET BODIES / EPOCH <date>`

### Segment 1 - Qualifying (2.0-11.0 s, frames 49-264)

- A threshold readout climbs. Objects below it turn grey, then fade out over 6 frames. The survivor count rolls down.
- Four heats, thresholds taken from `heats.json`. Heat 3 is chosen so the LEO shell dies in a single beat:
  - `HEAT 1  v > 7.6 km/s   <n> REMAIN`
  - `HEAT 2  v > 7.9 km/s   <n> REMAIN`  - the LEO shell vanishes here
  - `HEAT 3  v > 10.0 km/s  <n> REMAIN`
  - `FINAL   TOP 5`
- As the count drops, orbit trails switch on for survivors, once there are fewer than about 300. The long GTO ellipses appear.
- The camera pulls back slowly to fit the ellipses, out to about 40,000 km.

### Segment 2 - Finalists (11.0-14.0 s, frames 265-336)

- Five ellipses turn amber, everything else fades. The ISS circle appears in warm white, labelled `ISS / REFERENCE`.
- Name cards appear one by one, then hold: name, launch year, `v_p`. Five cards plus the ISS, in ladder form on the right.

### Segment 3 - Straighten (14.0-16.0 s, frames 337-384)

- The camera dives to perigee. Each finalist's perigee arc, and a matching arc of the ISS circle, unbends into a straight 100 m lane on the test range, the v2 range builder reused.
- Lanes 1-5 are the finalists in qualifying order. Lane 6 is the ISS. Lane 7 is world record pace.

### Segment 4 - The dash (16.0-23.0 s, frames 385-552)

- Side-on tracking shot. HUD: `SLOW MOTION <x>x`, with `x` computed so the winner's crossing lasts 5 s of screen time. Real elapsed time in ms on a big counter.
- Starter's pistol at the start line: one flash, then a sound ring expanding at 343 m/s in real time. On screen it creeps.
- The finalists cross. Each lane's finish time freezes on the board as it crosses.
- The ISS is still at ~73 m when the winner finishes.
- Lane 7 never moves. Label: `REACTION TIME NOT YET ELAPSED`.
- Sound ring label at the winner's finish: `SOUND OF PISTOL: <d> m`.

### Segment 5 - Results (23.0-28.0 s, frames 553-672)

- Results board, all from `race.json`:
  ```
  1  <NAME>      <t> ms
  2  <NAME>      <t> ms   +<gap> m
  ...
  6  ISS         <t> ms   +<gap> m
  7  WR PACE     DNS
  ```
- Optional photo-finish strip under the board: a time-on-x strip with each lane's crossing as a vertical tick.
- Board clears. Final card (section 0) in warm white, the last line in amber.
- Cut to black at frame 672.

The film must work without sound.

## 4. Blender implementation

### 4.1 Orbit points in Geometry Nodes

- One mesh object holds one vertex per object in the field. Per-vertex attributes are set from `field.json` at build time: `a`, `e`, `i`, `raan`, `argp`, `M0`, `n`, `v_p`, `rank`.
- A Geometry Nodes tree, built by the script, computes each position from scene time:
  - `M = M0 + n * t`
  - solve Kepler's equation for `E` with a Repeat Zone (6 Newton iterations, Blender 4.x or later)
  - true anomaly, radius, perifocal position, then rotation by `argp`, `i`, `raan`
- The scale is Earth radius = 1 Blender unit, so there's no float trouble at GTO distances.
- `t` = screen time x time-lapse factor, from `timeline.json`.
- Elimination: a `threshold(frame)` curve from `heats.json` drives an `alive` value per point, fading over 6 frames. Points instance a tiny low-poly sphere. Size and emission come from `alive` and `rank`.
- Deterministic: position is a pure function of the frame. Render any frame out of order.

If the Repeat Zone gives trouble, fall back to precomputing positions in Python for the frames of Segments 0-2 and storing them as shape keys on a decimated field.

### 4.2 Trails

Curves generated in Python for survivors once fewer than 300 remain: 128 points per ellipse from the elements. Visibility tied to `alive`. Never trails for the full field.

### 4.3 Straighten morph

For each finalist and the ISS, a curve with two shape keys:
- key 0: the perigee arc in globe space, about 100 m of real arc, magnified
- key 1: a straight lane on the range

The arc is centred on perigee. The object marker rides the curve. The camera move and the morph share one easing curve.

### 4.4 Builders

| Builder | Notes |
|---|---|
| `build_globe` | Reused from v2. |
| `build_range` | Reused from v2. Seven lanes, start line, finish line, 10 m ticks. |
| `build_rocket_body` | Generic upper stage: cylinder, tank band, single engine bell. Length and diameter as parameters. One instance per finalist lane. |
| `build_iss` | Low-poly: truss beam, 8 solar wing pairs as thin planes, module cylinders, radiators. Silhouette level. |
| `build_sprinter` | Wireframe figure in a crouched start, built from capsules. Never moves. |
| `build_sound_ring` | Thin torus at the start line. Radius = 343 m/s x real elapsed time. |
| `build_photo_strip` | Optional. |

### 4.5 HUD

Same system as v2:
- hand-authored anchors in `hud_layout.json`
- counter rolls as pre-built text objects switched by keyframed `hide_render`, no frame handlers
- essential text at least 28 px tall at 1080p
- amber only for the finalists, heat changes and the last line

New elements: heat panel, survivor counter, threshold readout, lane labels, ms counter, slow-motion factor, results board.

### 4.6 Palette

Unchanged: near-black background, phosphor green linework, warm white text, muted green-grey secondary, amber sparingly. Eliminated objects go grey before they disappear.

## 7. Validation

The build fails if:
- `field.json` is older than the raw data, or the raw data is older than 7 days at final render
- any finalist has a perigee under 120 km or `e >= 1`
- any displayed number has no source in the data files or `sources.md`
- the total runs over 30 s
- essential text would render under 28 px

`rank.py` prints:
- field size after each filter
- heat thresholds and survivors
- the five finalists with `v_p`, perigee, apogee and launch year
- the race table

Read this table before building anything. If the story isn't in the data, change the heats, not the data.

## 9. Submission

### Short description (draft)

Every tracked piece of debris and spent rocket stage in Earth orbit, on its real orbit. Qualifying heats eliminate the slow ones until five remain. The surprise: nothing in low orbit makes the final, because the fastest objects near Earth are old upper stages diving through the bottom of long transfer ellipses. The five finalists and the ISS then race 100 m at perigee speed, in slow motion. The race is over in about 10 ms. The starter's pistol hasn't reached lane 2.

### Procedural workflow notes (draft)

Python scripts download public orbital elements, filter them and rank every object by perigee speed. The ranking defines the heats, the finalists, lane order, race times and the results board, all written to JSON. One Blender Python script builds the whole film from an empty scene. A script-generated Geometry Nodes tree propagates every object's orbit from its real Keplerian elements by solving Kepler's equation each frame, and fades objects out as the speed threshold rises. Finalist perigee arcs morph into straight lanes through shape keys built from the same elements. Globe, range, rocket stages, ISS, sprinter, sound ring and HUD are all procedural. No hand-set keyframes, no external models, textures or fonts.

### Asset and AI disclosure (draft, edit to match what actually happened)

Orbital data: CelesTrak (or Space-Track), used as input data and credited. No third-party visual assets. The specification and parts of the code were written with AI assistance (Claude). No AI-generated imagery or audio.
